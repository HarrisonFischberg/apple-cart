# Apple Cart audit, 2026-11-16

Branch `phase-4/build`. Read-only audit: no source changed, no tests run (the 299 passing baseline was given), the app,
the helper and all hardware writes were left alone. Nothing was reproduced on the machine: every finding below comes from
reading the code, so each "failing case" is a traced path, not an observed failure.

Order used: correct, safe, holds up, tested, fast, lean.

## Verdict

The root helper is in good shape. The parts that matter most for a root daemon that writes the SMC held up under a close
read: the lease is armed before every write, both a wall-clock and an uptime deadline end it, every exit (lease, signal,
client gone, sleep, start) restores, restores retry until they succeed, the XPC listener fails closed without a team ID
and checks the caller twice, inputs are clamped on the helper side, and the two SMC whitelists are narrow. I found no way
for a caller that passes the gate to do more than: cut the charger for at most 300 s per call, or hold the fans between
their own minimum and maximum for at most 300 s per call.

The problems are in the app-side rules around the helper, not in the helper: one battery-harming setting (finding 1), one
feature that misreads the plug while a limit is held (2), and three clipboard privacy gaps (3, 4, 5). Nothing here blocks
further building; 1 to 5 should be fixed before anyone other than the owner runs it.

17 findings: 1 must fix, 7 should fix, 9 nice to have.

## Must fix

### 1. A charge limit of 0% or 5% drains the battery to 5% and then switches the charger on and off forever

- File: `Packages/AppleCartKit/Sources/Core/ChargeLimit.swift:8`, `:11`, `:115`; `Core/ChargeLimiter.swift:202`;
  `HelperProtocol/ChargeLimits.swift:2-8`
- What this is: the allowed range for the charge limit, and the rule that never holds the charger off at or below 5%.
- Problem: `ChargeLimitSettings.range` is `0...100` and the limit popover offers a `0%` chip (the Settings text field
  also takes 5). `HelperProtocol.ChargeLimits` says the product range is 20 to 100 and that "the helper clamps again",
  but nothing outside `LimitsTests` uses it: the helper never sees a limit, and the app never applies that range.
  Failing case: plugged in at 60%, pick 0%. `decide` returns hold, the helper cuts the charger, the Mac runs on the
  battery down to 5%. At 5% the emergency floor says charge; at 6% the limit says hold again. There is no resume band at
  the floor, so the charger is cut and restored every few minutes for as long as the Mac is plugged in. Each restore is
  also the step the spike saw stall ("charging did not resume until a replug"). On top of that, line 202 (the 60 s
  minimum hold) turns the floor's "charge now" back into a hold when the limit is 0 or 5, because `5 > limit - 3`.
- Fix: make the app use the documented range: `ChargeLimitSettings.range = 20...100`, drop `0` from `choices`, and add
  `seen.limitLevel > ChargePolicy.emergencyFloor` to the condition on line 202. If limits below 20 are wanted on
  purpose, instead give the floor its own band (hold only above `emergencyFloor + 5`) and delete the unused
  `ChargeLimits` enum so the code stops claiming a clamp that does not exist. Add one limiter test at limit 0.
- If we skip it: one tap parks a plugged-in Mac at 5 to 6% with the charger cycling all day, in an app whose job is
  battery care. An unplug at that point is a shutdown within minutes.

## Should fix

### 2. Keep Awake reads the raw plug state, which is wrong while a charge limit is being held

- File: `Packages/AppleCartKit/Sources/Core/KeepAwake.swift:128-134` (reader wired at `:86`)
- What this is: Keep Awake's "end when unplugged", "re-enable sleep at N%" and "until the charge limit is reached".
- Problem: it calls `BatteryReader.read()` directly. The spike (`docs/spikes/charge-control.md` item 3) found that with
  the charger cut, macOS reports battery power while the charger is still in. The limiter and the sampler both correct
  for this (adapter details, `cutHidesPlug`); Keep Awake does not. Failing case A: limit 80%, Keep Awake on with "end on
  unplug". The limiter starts holding, the next 10 s check reads "not plugged in", Keep Awake stops with "Charger
  unplugged" although nothing was unplugged. Failing case B: "until the limit is reached" needs `isPluggedIn` to be
  true at the moment the level reaches the limit; once the hold starts it reads false, so if the limiter's level gets
  there first the session never ends and the Mac never sleeps. Case C: with a hold active, "re-enable sleep at N%"
  treats the Mac as on battery. Likely, not reproduced.
- Fix: in `shouldStop`, use the same rule the sampler uses: `let plugged = r.isPluggedIn || r.adapterWatts != nil`, and
  use `plugged` in the three checks. One line plus one test with a reading that has adapter details but
  `isPluggedIn == false`.
- If we skip it: Keep Awake and the charge limit, which the parity plan sells as working together, quietly break each
  other.

### 3. Deleting a clipboard screenshot leaves the image file on disk

- File: `Packages/AppleCartKit/Sources/Core/Clipboard.swift:191-195`, `:221-223`; `UI/ClipboardView.swift:73`, `:81`
- What this is: Delete on one clipboard item, with Undo.
- Problem: `removing` keeps the deleted items in `lastDeleted` so Undo can bring them back, and `prune` refuses to delete
  any image file that `lastDeleted` still names. `lastDeleted` is only emptied by Undo, by the next delete, or by
  "Erase all". Failing case: copy a screenshot of a password, open the panel, Delete it. The PNG stays in
  `~/Library/Application Support/Apple Cart/Clipboard/` until the app restarts or something else is deleted, which on a
  menu bar app can be days. The only Undo button is on the Settings page, so most people never see why.
- Fix: end the undo window by itself. In `removing`, start a task that calls `discardUndo()` after about 10 s if
  `lastDeleted` is still the same items (the class already takes an injected `sleep`), and also call `discardUndo()` in
  `stop()`.
- If we skip it: "Delete" does not delete, for the one kind of item most likely to be sensitive.

### 4. "Never store from this app" judges by whichever app is in front up to half a second later

- File: `App/ClipboardSupport.swift:14`; `Packages/AppleCartKit/Sources/Core/Clipboard.swift:96-97`, `:117`
- What this is: the per-app exclusion list for clipboard history.
- Problem: the pasteboard does not say who wrote to it, so the snapshot uses `frontmostApplication` at poll time, and
  the poll runs every 0.5 s. Failing case: an excluded password app that does not mark its copies as concealed. The
  owner copies a password and switches to the browser straight away (the normal thing to do). If the switch lands
  before the next poll, the source is recorded as the browser and the password is saved to `history.json` in plain
  text. Copies made from a menu bar popup are always attributed to the app behind it.
- Fix: listen for `NSWorkspace.didActivateApplicationNotification`, remember the previous front app and when it changed,
  and skip the item if either the current app or the app that was in front within the last poll interval is excluded.
  Say in the Settings text that this goes by the app in front when you copy.
- If we skip it: the exclusion list gives a promise it only keeps some of the time.

### 5. Screen lock wipes the clipboard even when the Clipboard tool is off

- File: `App/AppDelegate.swift:91-93`; `Packages/AppleCartKit/Sources/Core/Clipboard.swift:150-153`
- What this is: "clear the clipboard automatically" (after 60 s and on screen lock).
- Problem: the lock observer is registered at launch for every user and `screenLocked()` only checks the saved
  `autoClear` setting, not whether the tool is running. `stop()` cancels the 60 s timer but not this. Failing case: turn
  auto clear on, later turn the Clipboard tool off in Settings. Every screen lock still empties the system clipboard.
- Fix: `guard autoClear, isPolling else { return }` in `screenLocked()`.
- If we skip it: a tool that is switched off keeps changing the system. It reads as a bug in macOS to the person it
  happens to.

### 6. The charger cut is never read back, so the app can say "Holding" when nothing is held

- File: `Packages/AppleCartKit/Sources/HelperCore/AdapterGuard.swift:97-100`
- What this is: the helper's "charger off" write.
- Problem: `FanGuard` reads every write back and returns `notApplied` if it did not stick. `AdapterGuard` returns `.ok`
  as soon as the SMC accepts the write. Failing case: a macOS update or another Mac where the SMC accepts `CHIE = 08`
  with result 0 but ignores it. The helper answers ok, the app shows "Holding at 80%", and the battery keeps charging.
  The 30 s status poll then notices, drops the claim, the next pass cuts again and the claim comes back: the panel
  alternates between "Holding" and "Charging" while the limit is never enforced.
- Fix: after the write on line 98, `guard try read() == SMCWhitelist.adapterOff else { restore; return .notApplied }`
  (the error code already exists). One fake-SMC test.
- If we skip it: the project's own rule ("the UI never claims a limit that is not being held") depends on the SMC
  never changing its mind.

### 7. The fan watchdog can declare "the fan did not respond" when the curve moved the target

- File: `Packages/AppleCartKit/Sources/Core/FanRunner.swift:281`, `:298-310`
- What this is: the check that every fan is within 20% of the target 15 s after a manual speed starts.
- Problem: the 15 s window starts once, at the first apply, but it is checked against whatever the target is now.
  Failing case: Curve applied while the Mac is warming up. First target 2,950 rpm at t = 0; at t = 10 s the curve moves
  to 5,300 rpm. At t = 15 s the fan has had 5 s to climb, reads about 4,000, fails the 20% test, and the runner hands
  back, shows "The fan did not respond" and refuses to do anything until Apply is pressed again, exactly when cooling
  was wanted. The live test only covered a steady Min target; how fast the fan climbs to a high target is listed in
  `docs/fan-plan.md` as not verified.
- Fix: restart the window when the target changes while it is still open. Line 281 becomes
  `if sentRPM == nil || (verifySince != nil && sentRPM != rpm) { verifySince = now() }`. One test.
- If we skip it: Curve mode can switch itself off under rising load and stay off.

### 8. `ps` is started every few seconds, all day, for a list nobody is looking at

- File: `Packages/AppleCartKit/Sources/Core/Battery.swift:274-284` (callers `:199`, `:205`)
- What this is: the "apps using significant energy" list on the Battery screen.
- Problem: every timer sample launches `/bin/ps -Ao ...` over every process. The sampler runs whenever anything holds
  it, and the charge limiter (any limit below 100, Top Up or heat protection) and the menu bar readout both hold it
  permanently, at the menu bar interval (2 to 20 s). The result is only shown in `BatteryView`. So with a charge limit
  set, the app forks a process every 5 s, around 17,000 times a day, with the panel closed.
- Fix: only collect the list while the Battery screen is open: in `sample`, `guard withEnergyUsers,
  holders.contains("batteryView") else { return }`.
- If we skip it: a battery-care app spends energy around the clock on something invisible, and it shows up in its own
  energy list.

## Nice to have

### 9. Fan restore checks the mode straight after writing it, with no settle time

- File: `Packages/AppleCartKit/Sources/HelperCore/FanGuard.swift:319`
- What this is: handing the fans back to macOS and confirming the mode key reads 0.
- Problem: `setFan` waits 150 ms before reading back because, in the file's own words, the SMC applies a mode change
  asynchronously. The restore path reads back at once. On this Mac it passed live. On a Mac where the mode lags, every
  restore would report failure: the app shows "Could not set the fans", a retry timer runs, and a SIGTERM exit returns
  1 so launchd restarts the helper.
- Fix: call `settle()` before the read-back loop on line 319, only on the path that actually wrote.
- If we skip it: possible false "failed" reports on two-fan or other-chip Macs, which are already listed as unverified.

### 10. The helper accepts any binary signed by the team with the app's identifier, including development builds

- File: `Packages/AppleCartKit/Sources/HelperProtocol/HelperXPC.swift:75`
- What this is: the code-signing requirement the helper checks on every caller.
- Problem: `anchor apple generic and identifier ... and certificate leaf[subject.OU] = team` is satisfied by an Apple
  Development build, which carries `get-task-allow`, so any process of the same user can attach to it and drive the
  helper. Today only the owner's Mac has such builds, and all a caller can do is cut the charger or set fans inside
  their range for up to 300 s, so the exposure is small.
- Fix: in Release builds append ` and certificate leaf[field.1.2.840.113635.100.6.1.13] exists` (Developer ID
  Application) to the requirement; keep the current string for Debug.
- If we skip it: every build ever signed by the team stays a valid key to the root helper.

### 11. After an app update the old helper keeps running unless someone remembers to bump the version

- File: `Packages/AppleCartKit/Sources/HelperProtocol/HelperXPC.swift:12`; `App/Updater.swift`
- What this is: how the app decides the installed helper is out of date.
- Problem: the check is the hand-edited `HelperIdentity.version`. Sparkle replaces the app bundle, but the running
  daemon keeps executing the old code until a reboot or Repair. A fix inside `FanGuard` or `AdapterGuard` that does not
  change the XPC surface ships to nobody unless the number is also bumped.
- Fix: have the helper report its `CFBundleVersion` in the `version` reply's place for the "outdated" check, or add a
  release-checklist line: any change under `HelperCore/` or `Helper/` bumps `HelperIdentity.version`.
- If we skip it: a safety fix to the root helper can sit undelivered while the app says "Installed and working".

### 12. Any client going away restores for everyone

- File: `Packages/AppleCartKit/Sources/HelperCore/HelperService.swift:95-98`
- What this is: "the app went away, so restore now" on connection invalidation.
- Problem: it fires for every connection. Failing case: a second copy of the app (a Debug build next to the installed
  one) starts and quits; the helper restores the charger and the fans under the first copy, which notices within 30 s
  and re-applies. Fail-safe, but a flap.
- Fix: count accepted connections and restore only when the last one goes.
- If we skip it: only matters with two copies running; mostly the owner while developing.

### 13. The battery temperature from the battery entry is not range-checked

- File: `Packages/AppleCartKit/Sources/Core/Battery.swift:63`
- What this is: the temperature heat protection acts on.
- Problem: the SMC path filters to 1 to 90 C (`BatterySensors.temperature`). The registry path takes
  `Temperature / 100` as is and wins when present. A bogus high value on some Mac would make heat protection pause
  charging every five minutes down to the 20% heat floor.
- Fix: apply the same `(1...90).contains` filter on line 63.
- If we skip it: a bad sensor value on another Mac looks like "Apple Cart will not charge my Mac".

### 14. "Delete all data" writes a setting back and leaves the root helper installed

- File: `App/AppDelegate.swift:194-202`; `Packages/AppleCartKit/Sources/Core/FanRunner.swift:230-232`
- What this is: the Delete all data button.
- Problem: after `eraseAll()` the app calls `terminate`, which runs `fanRunner.shutdown()` again, which writes
  `fan.active` into the just-erased preferences (the window frame autosave does the same). The root helper stays
  registered. Neither is personal data.
- Fix: skip the second shutdown once data was erased (a flag checked in `applicationShouldTerminate`), and either
  unregister the helper there or say in the alert that the helper stays until removed in Settings.
- If we skip it: "removes every setting" is not quite true, and a root daemon outlives the data wipe unannounced.

### 15. Cleaning Mode leaks one event tap per use

- File: `App/BreaksSupport.swift:75-80`
- What this is: the keyboard and trackpad block while wiping the keyboard.
- Problem: `stop()` disables the tap and drops the reference but never calls `CFMachPortInvalidate`, so each session
  leaves a disabled tap and its Mach port behind until quit.
- Fix: `CFMachPortInvalidate(tap)` in `stop()`.
- If we skip it: a small leak per use; harmless in practice.

### 16. Copying a large image converts it on the main thread before the size limit is checked

- File: `App/ClipboardSupport.swift:12`; `Packages/AppleCartKit/Sources/Core/Clipboard.swift:128`
- What this is: the pasteboard snapshot for clipboard history.
- Problem: a TIFF on the pasteboard is turned into PNG on the main actor for every copy, and only then compared with
  the 10 MB limit. Copying a large image freezes the menu bar app for the length of the encode and throws the result
  away.
- Fix: skip the conversion when the TIFF data is already over `maxImageBytes`.
- If we skip it: an occasional beach ball in the panel after copying big images.

### 17. The app target has no tests, and it holds the XPC client

- File: `App/HelperChargeController.swift:55-65`, `:138-146`; `App/FanTool.swift`, `App/BatteryTool.swift`
- What this is: the one connection to the root helper (5 s timeout, resume-exactly-once) and the off-then-on ordering
  of the two tools that drive it.
- Problem: all of it lives in `App/`, which no test target builds. The package tests cover the policy with fakes, so a
  mistake in the timeout, the reply mapping (`command`) or the stop/start ordering would only show on real hardware.
- Fix: move `HelperClient`'s `Once` and the reply-code mapping into `Core` (they need no AppKit) and give each one
  test. Not a new test target.
- If we skip it: the thinnest, most timing-sensitive layer stays verified by hand only.

## Checked and found sound (no finding)

- Lease: armed before the write, generation-guarded, wall-clock and uptime timers, checked again on every call
  (`expireIfDueLocked`), clamped to 10 to 300 s on the helper side.
- Restore paths: helper start, SIGTERM/SIGINT (non-zero exit on failure so launchd restarts it), connection
  invalidated or interrupted, system will sleep (and cuts refused until power-on, with a 60 s escape), failed cut,
  failed fan apply. Failed restores retry and are capped at 60 s while a cut may be live.
- XPC: listener-level requirement plus per-connection requirement plus a second check by pid; no requirement means no
  connection; the app pins the helper's identity too. Replies are plain integers and booleans.
- SMC: charger writes limited to `CHIE` 00 or 08; fan writes limited to mode 0/1, `Ftst` 0/1 and a finite target of 1 to
  10,000 rpm after clamping to the fan's own range; key type and size checked before any fan write; NaN and infinite
  values are filtered on both the helper and the app side before any integer conversion.
- Limiter: never starts or renews a cut from a reading older than 15 s, restores when the helper is not ready, handles a
  tool-off that lands during a command, checks that charging resumes.
- Fan policy: hottest plausible sensor, no reading means macOS, 90 C release with a 2 minute lockout and an 80 C latch.
- Uninstaller and Cleaner: symlinks resolved, fixed roots, Trash with undo, Apple apps and the app itself refused.
- Flush DNS: the admin prompt runs one fixed command; nothing user-supplied reaches the shell string.
- Sparkle: off until a signing key exists; HTTPS feed; the framework download is pinned by SHA-256.
